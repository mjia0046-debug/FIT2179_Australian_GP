Australian Grand Prix: global race, local impact

Thirty-eight races. Two cities. One global sport. How a southern-hemisphere round of Formula 1 became Victoria's biggest sporting export.

01

A global sport lands in Australia

F1 circuits form a global map. Australia is highlighted through Adelaide and Melbourne.

Global F1 circuits

F1 started in Europe in 1950 and has since grown into a genuinely global series. Australia is a long way from that original heartland, yet Adelaide and Melbourne have been on the calendar for four decades. Larger dots = more races held at that circuit.

Australian GP host eras

Adelaide had 11 races (1985–95), then Melbourne took over and ran 24 straight (1996–2019). COVID wiped out 2020 and 2021. Melbourne picked up again in 2022.

Takeaway

The Australian GP is not some isolated local event — it's how Australia plugs into a racing series that touches six continents.

02

Home drivers and global champions

Australian drivers carry the home-race story; international champions shape the race record.

Australian home drivers ▶ Click a name

From Brabham in the 1950s to Piastri today, 19 Australians have raced in F1 — though only two ever won the championship. Click a name to see stats.

Top non-Australian drivers ▶ Switch metric ↑

Metric switch: Wins = victories; Podiums = P1-P3; Top 10 = P1-P10 finishes.

Schumacher leads with 4 wins in Melbourne; Vettel and Button each have 3. Use the selector to flip between wins, podiums, and top-10 finishes — the ranking shuffles quite a bit depending on the metric.

Australian driver career timeline

Grey bars show each driver's career span; red dots mark home-GP appearances. The coloured band at the top shows which era (Adelaide / Melbourne / Pause / Return) each year falls into.

Takeaway

Local drivers give the crowd someone to cheer for, even though the race winners are almost always from overseas.

03

Winning the Australian GP is international history

Click a country tile to reveal the winning drivers behind that country's Australian GP record.

Where do the winners come from?

Each arc connects a winning country to Melbourne — thicker = more wins. 10 of the 11 nationalities are European, with Brazil the sole non-European winner.

Champion nationalities and winning drivers ▶ Click a country

The UK and Germany have the most wins, but 11 nationalities appear overall. Click a country to drill down — e.g. the UK's 11 wins are spread across six different drivers, not one superstar.

Takeaway

Wins are concentrated in a few nations, but look closer and each total comes from different drivers, teams, and decades — no single dynasty owns this race.

04

Constructor legacy and dominance

Every point is a race year; the treemap shows each constructor's share of Australian GP wins.

AUS-shaped constructor track + treemap ▶ Hover or click a team

Follow the AUS-shaped path year by year — each dot is one race winner, coloured by team. The treemap underneath totals up wins per constructor. Ferrari, McLaren, and Mercedes each dominated different periods.

Takeaway

Team-level history is a story of eras: Williams and McLaren in the 90s, Ferrari in the 2000s, Mercedes after 2014. No one team holds this race permanently.

05

The spectacle becomes economic impact

Since the post-pandemic return, visitors, economic contribution, and supported jobs have grown.

Post-pandemic growth at a glance

All four metrics are set to 100 at 2022 so you can compare growth rates directly. Overseas visitors jumped the most (+221%), then FTE jobs (+122%) and spending (+89%). Total unique visitors grew a steadier +31%.

Visitor origin

Once the race came back, visitor numbers climbed every year. Interstate fans make up the bulk, but overseas visitors tripled — from 6,471 in 2022 to 20,792 in 2025, pushing their share from ~9% up to 21%.

Direct expenditure and GSP

Visitor spending (hotels, food, transport, tickets) nearly doubled from $92M to $174M. The broader GSP contribution — Victoria's share of economic activity linked to the race — went from $171M to $324M over the same period.

FTE jobs supported

FTE jobs tied to the race more than doubled — 734 in 2022 up to 1,631 in 2025. That's hospitality, security, transport, media production, and everything else that keeps a major event running.

Overall message

Three threads run through this story: the race connects Australia to a worldwide sport; it creates local memories via home drivers, overseas champions, and team rivalries; and since the pandemic, it has become a serious economic engine for Victoria — bringing tourists, spending, and jobs that keep growing year on year.

Takeaway

Since coming back in 2022, every key number has gone up — overseas visitors tripled and FTE jobs more than doubled, while total attendance rose 31% and spending grew 89%.
